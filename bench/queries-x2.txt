# X2 (ticket 0014, SPEC.md §5.3) — the query population for the stopword-less OR p95.
#
# What this measures. Deleting the stoplist means every one of these 29 English words
# now reaches the OR-query instead of being thrown away:
#
#   the a an and or of to in on for with is are was were be by as at that this it from
#   we our their its these those
#
# They are the highest-document-frequency terms in an English corpus, so they are exactly
# the postings the deletion asks FTS5 to walk. The cost of that walk is the one thing to
# check before shipping the deletion, and it is what this file exercises.
#
# How it is built. Twenty natural-language queries a researcher would actually type in
# this library — climate and energy economics, the history of economic thought, Vietnam —
# each carrying at least three of the words above, several carrying six or more. They are
# questions, not keyword stacks, because the figure has to describe what a user pays.
#
# `a` is not exercised on its own: `tokenize()` keeps its one-character drop, so `a`
# never reaches the query either way. The other 28 do.
#
# Line 4 is the PR's own example, kept because it is the case that tokenized to nothing
# at all before the deletion — every one of its words was on the list.
#
# Committed rather than written to /tmp: the query population is provenance. A p95 is a
# statement about a population, and a population nobody can reproduce makes the figure
# unfalsifiable.
