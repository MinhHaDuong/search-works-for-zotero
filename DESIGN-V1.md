# THE SETTLED LEDGER
**The synthesis architecture for zoteus search — one design, assembled from six, corrected by their critics.**

Everything below was written after re-verifying the load-bearing code claims in `/home/user/zoteus-ci/src/features/search/` this session: the query path really does destroy non-ASCII terms (`tokenize.ts:8` is `/[a-z0-9]+/g` plus 29 English stopwords, called from `sqlite-index.ts:430`); the driver really is synchronous (`DatabaseSync`, `sqlite-index.ts:17,106`); the FTS tokenizer is *already* `unicode61 remove_diacritics 2` (`sqlite-index.ts:159`); `schemaVersion` is stamped (`:164`) but never read back or compared (`loadMeta`, `:221-235` — the "rebuild-on-old-schema rule" does not exist); `cosine()` recomputes the row norm on every row of every query (`:526`); the corruption message tells a shell-less MCP agent to run `rm` (`corruption.ts:77-78`); the default local embedder is English-ish `Xenova/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (`embeddings.ts:145`); RRF k=60 is already upstream (`index-manager.ts:88`). Where the architects and critics disagreed, these facts decided.

## 1. The skeleton: The Ledger Line's frame, and why it beat its rivals

The skeleton is **The Ledger Line**: a durable **(item × stage) ledger in SQLite** — explicit rows carrying `input_key`/`output_key`/`status`/`priority`/`lease`, drained by idempotent claim→compute→commit loops, with the pipeline living **outside the server process** and coordinating through **two deliberately different planes** (durable work through the database; latency-sensitive control through a stdio pipe). Crash-safety, incrementality, invalidation, and coverage reporting all fall out of one table.

Why this frame and not the others — the critiques decided it:

- **The Ledger** is the same church with a stricter religion, and the critic demolished exactly the two commitments that distinguish it. Queues-as-pure-views fail arithmetic at the design point (O(N) anti-join per 64-row claim, ~O(N²/batch) for a full migration, an O(650k) `COUNT(*)` in every reply), and its own named fallback — a materialized dirty flag — is, in its own words, "quietly admitting queues-as-tables again." Worse, its co-resident workers sit on the **verified synchronous driver**: an in-server chunker committing a monster doc blocks the very event loop that must answer in 3 seconds, and its scheduling signal is priority-inverted (foreground must win the contended write lock just to write the row telling background to yield). The Ledger Line's materialized status rows and its process boundary are precisely the fixes for both.
- **The Two-Log Replica** contributes the best crawl story in the field (grafted below) but loses as a frame: no failure policy anywhere (one poison item silently stalls all coverage growth forever — the exact state R1 forbids), a flagship migration promise that is impossible in FTS5 (the tokenizer is table-scoped; "key bump drained newest-first" is actually a whole-table rebuild), and its refusal to store extracted text makes its own R3 story expensive (every re-chunk is a re-download that stalls when Zotero is closed).
- **Five Patches** is an on-ramp, not a frame — and its critic showed two of its five PRs stood on evidence that was absent (the schema-migration rule I verified today does not exist), misattributed (the 404MB figure was measured *with* the 40k cap the PR removes; uncapped measured 1.85GB), or arithmetically refuted (the "memory-bandwidth-bound" premise: 121ms at 100k float32 is ~1.4GB/s effective — per-row-overhead-bound, so int8 *in that layout* buys roughly nothing). Its increments and its NULL-vector queue are grafted; its architectural role ends there.
- **Position One** and **MAÎTRE D'** are layers, not machinery — a ranking layer and a contract layer — and both had their headline numbers destroyed (the query-path embedder's RAM unlocated in either budget; bge-m3's 1024 dims silently quadrupling every scan and memory figure; `frontierDate` false by construction because per-stage frontiers provably diverge). Their layers survive and are grafted on top of the skeleton, where they belong.

What the critic broke in the Ledger Line itself, the synthesis repairs rather than inherits: the depth-major embed order (breadth-first, breaking R2 and making the coverage sentence unstatable) becomes recency-major with a depth cap (§3.4); the arithmetic errors (62MB vs 250MB int8, ~40MB vs ~118MB model) are corrected everywhere numbers appear; the backpressure deadlock and mass-quarantine-on-unreachability get a real failure policy (§2.6); query embedding moves out of the nice-19 process (§2.3); and four processes become two.

## 2. The architecture, with every graft named

### 2.1 Storage: one self-describing file plus one derived sidecar

`search-index.sqlite` (WAL, `synchronous=NORMAL`, `busy_timeout=4000`, page cache capped 32–64MB): items + facets (`item_tags`, `item_collections`, collections tree), the **ledger** (`item_key × stage` rows with `input_key`, `output_key`, `status ∈ pending|leased|done|empty|failed|quarantined`, `priority`, `cursor`, `lease_until`, `attempts`, `error`), **slabs** (gzipped ≤1MiB segments of extracted text — graft: Ledger Line's stored slabs / The Ledger's `text_segments`; this is what makes a chunker-geometry change a CPU-only event with zero Zotero requests, and what the Two-Log Replica's critique proved you cannot do without), **passages** (external-content FTS5, two columns `meta_text`/`body_text` weighted `bm25(fts, 2.0, 1.0)` — graft: Position One's field weighting), and **vectors** keyed `(pid, embedder_key)` per row (graft: The Ledger).

Passages carry `item_key` and nothing else denormalized. This deliberately kills The Ledger's stale-denormalization defect class (its critic's R5 finding): filters are resolved item-side at query time into an allowed-pid bitmap (§2.7), so there is no copied `item_type`/`date` column to go stale.

`vectors-<embedderKey>.i8` (or `.f32`, per experiment X1): a **derived, rebuildable sidecar** — header with magic, embedder identity, dim, count; rowid array; per-vector scale; contiguous matrix — regenerated from the SQLite blobs by a compaction pass, deletable without loss (graft: Position One's sidecar-as-cache, promoted to primary per The Ledger's critic: "the slab cache must be primary, not fallback").

Two watermarks — `item_watermark` and `fulltext_watermark`, with a backend label — live as two differently-named meta rows that no code path can transpose, written in the same transaction as the data they describe (graft: The Ledger; enforced-by-schema per MAÎTRE D's "sequence segregation by type"). The 0012 defect class becomes unwritable.

**Key design of the derivation keys** (fixing both critics' R3 findings at once): the extract stage is keyed *only* by the per-attachment `/fulltext?since=` counter (the observable extractor proxy, C1); the metadata-chunk stage by `item_version` + chunker key; the fulltext-chunk stage by (slab hash, chunker key); embed by (passage presence in the sidecar under the current embedder key) — the **NULL-vector-is-the-queue** idea (graft: Five Patches, promoted to the only embed path, which also cures its own monster-doc frontier stall). Consequences: a tag or collection edit touches facet tables only (no re-fetch of a 45MB monster — Ledger Line's critique R3 closed); a title edit re-chunks one item's metadata passages only (MAÎTRE D's critique R3 closed); a re-extraction re-does one attachment's chain only. Items with no extracted text get an `empty` tombstone row so coverage counts them, and their metadata passages are indexed regardless (graft: pr6012's NULL-row via Ledger Line, with its critic's keyword-regression caveat closed).

### 2.2 Discovery: the census-seeded, newest-first frontier

Graft: the Two-Log Replica's **ledger/materializer split** — the only design that reconciled "replication client" with "newest first," per its own critic. One `?format=versions` census (~20 bytes/item; ~200KB at 10k) seeds the complete universe of keys+versions up front — including attachment keys, closing The Ledger's attachment-deletion hole — then a metadata sweep paged `sort=dateAdded&direction=desc` (verified whitelisted in the local API) fills item rows newest-first. Completeness comes from the census (any key never fetched stays on the frontier — offset-pagination drift, Five Patches' critique R1, cannot lose an item); order comes from the sorted sweep; drift self-heals through the `?since=` delta. Deletions are census-subtraction every Nth tick — the one honestly O(library) cost the API forces, disclosed as such. A backwards library version (backup restore) heals by census diff, not wipe: artifacts whose stored versions still match survive untouched (graft: Two-Log's census-diff healing, replacing all four rebuild-on-mismatch branches in the fork's `delta.ts`).

### 2.3 Topology: two OS processes, three loops, two planes

**P0, the server**: answers queries; runs the reconcile tick (small HTTP + tiny transactions); holds the **query-side embedding session** at normal priority. This last point is the committed fix for the failure both Position One's and MAÎTRE D's critics found: query embedding is foreground work and lives in the foreground process — never behind IPC to a nice-19 child (priority inversion), never unbudgeted. The model is the 384-dim multilingual-e5-small class, ~112–120MB at q8 (the honest figure, not Ledger Line's ~40MB; and explicitly not bge-m3 — MAÎTRE D's dimension trap). Cold path: the first semantic query that finds the model unloaded serves keyword-only immediately with a typed `EMBEDDER_WARMING` degradation and warms in the background — the 3s budget is never spent on model load (Position One's cold-path critique closed).

**P1, the pipeline worker**: one child process, `nice 19` (IDLE class on Windows), running extract→chunk→embed as three independently-paced loops over the ledger. Two processes, not the hint's three-plus-supervisor: the OS-process boundary is needed *once* — between pipeline and server — to deliver nice/observe/kill (owner justification b), while stage decoupling (justification a: keyword availability never waits on embedding) comes from the ledger's queues regardless of process count. Ledger Line's own weakness list called its chunk process "a thin stage paying process overhead for containment it will rarely need"; leases already make SIGKILL free, so containment is not worth ~3× Node baseline. Because claims are lease-based, splitting embed into its own process later is a deployment flag, not a redesign.

**Two planes** (graft: Ledger Line): durable work through DB claim/commit — the one-statement atomic lease claim with expired-lease theft and a `claimed_input` guard on commit (grafts: The Ledger's claim statement + Two-Log's optimistic version-stamped completion); control through the stdio pipe — `pause`, `resume`, `shutdown`. **The ONE scheduLING RULE'S mechanism**: the server sends `pause` down the pipe when a query arrives (microseconds, no lock) and `resume` after ~2s idle; the worker checks between micro-batches (embed batches of 8–32, chunk commits every ~500 passages), so pause latency is bounded by one micro-batch. Crucially, **the query path writes nothing to the database** — it is a pure WAL reader plus a pipe write — which structurally eliminates The Ledger's priority inversion rather than mitigating it. `nice 19` remains the always-on floor beneath the cooperative layer. (Cooperative self-SIGSTOP: rejected — Unix-only complexity buying little once micro-batches bound the pause, and SIGKILL is already free.)

Monster documents: the worker fetches one attachment's body (the local API returns one JSON string — O(document) at the fetch boundary is unreachable-around without upstream Zotero surgery, stated plainly per Ledger Line's honest fencing and Five Patches' critique R9), splits it into ≤1MiB gzipped slabs while streaming, frees, then chunks slab-at-a-time with an overlap tail and a resume `cursor` — a 15k-page doc is ~700 independently committed units. The O(document) transient (~100–200MB for the measured 44.9MB doc) is fenced inside the expendable worker with an RSS watchdog: kill at 500MB → retry → quarantine with a named reason that appears in search replies.

### 2.4 Fairness: recency-major, depth-capped

Ledger Line's depth-major frontier is rejected on its critic's proof (breadth-first: the oldest item's passage 1 before the newest item's passage 2 — R2 inverted, the coverage sentence unstatable, and the claimed idf benefit a category error since idf is set at FTS-insertion time). The refix follows the critic's own prescription: **two bands**. Band 0: the first **K=64** passages of every item, in recency order; band 1: overflow beyond K, also recency order; band 0 drains first. K=64 is principled — measured density is 63 passages/item, so band 0 is "one typical item's worth": ~99% of items get *fully* embedded strictly newest-first, monsters get their head (title, abstract, opening chapters — the retrieval-valuable part) at their proper recency slot, and a monster's tail becomes the background of the background instead of a ~19-minute monopoly (MAÎTRE D's critique R2). Keyword/chunk order stays strictly newest-first — chunking is cheap and needs no fairness machinery.

### 2.5 Freshness: the tick owns the clock; the query path owns one probe

Graft: Ledger Line's supervisor-owned freshness + the fork's `delta.ts` protocol, with Five Patches' fatal hole closed. The **reconcile tick** (in P0: 60s idle cadence, 10-minute backoff while Zotero is unreachable) sweeps **both logs on its own clock** — `?since=item_watermark`, and the `/fulltext?since=` ascending completed-version-group sweep — so re-extraction reaches the index even on a metadata-quiescent library (Five Patches' probe watched only `libraryVersion`; a clock-driven tick over both sequences makes that hole unconstructible). The **query path** does zero Zotero requests when the tick ran within ~30s; otherwise exactly one memoized version probe with a 500ms deadline, which *reports and nudges, never awaits* — probe-don't-fix (graft: MAÎTRE D's phrasing; the fork's 8s inline apply is deleted as "an 8s deadline inside a 3s budget is a category error"). Replies carry `probedMsAgo` and the indexed-vs-current versions of both sequences.

### 2.6 The failure policy every design lacked

Synthesized from the R1 findings of four critics, this is new and non-optional:

- Errors are classified **transient** (unreachable, timeout, 5xx) vs **persistent** (deterministic failure on the same input). Transient errors never count against an item and never quarantine — reachability gates claiming globally, so an overnight-closed Zotero pauses the pipeline instead of mass-quarantining it (Ledger Line's critique R1).
- Persistent failures: attempts with backoff, quarantine at 3 for extract/chunk; embed batches quarantine by **bisection** — split and re-enqueue until the single poison passage is isolated with its error (graft: Ledger Line, log2(batch) cost).
- Quarantined rows **leave the frontier** (no head-of-line stall — Two-Log's fatal defect) but are named in coverage and status; quarantine **auto-clears when the row's input key changes** (item edited, re-extracted, artifact key bumped) and a manual `retry` verb exists.
- Backpressure is counted in *items awaiting chunk* (pause extraction above ~4), with quarantined rows excluded — the 32MB-bytes threshold that a single 44.9MB monster could deadlock (Ledger Line's critique) is unrepresentable.

Convergence is then a statement, not a hope: every state — empty, mid-build, crashed, capped, corrupt (sidelined, never deleted: renamed `.corrupt-<ts>` for autopsy, fresh file, auto-build, notice — graft: Five Patches), stale, backwards, wrong-geometry, wrong-embedder, poisoned — drains to 100% coverage or to a *named* quarantine visible in every reply. "Rebuild" is a state (mark-everything-stale), not a verb (graft: Two-Log).

### 2.7 Query path and ranking

Filters (`collection` expanded recursively, `tags`, `itemType`, `year range`) compile item-side into an allowed-pid **bitmap** consulted by both engines *before* scoring — the FTS statement joins the predicate down; the sidecar scan skips non-allowed rows — so a filtered query costs less than an unfiltered one and top-k is never post-filtered (grafts: Position One's bitmap, The Ledger's filters-before-the-dot-product, Five Patches' pre-admission enforcement at the backend signature).

Ranking: each engine's passage list is **collapsed to items before ranks are assigned** (item score = best passage; best passage kept for the snippet) — the structural fix for the measured 0013 symptom, one rank per 42,963-passage dictionary per engine (graft: Position One). Fusion stays **plain RRF k=60**, tie-shared — deliberately identical to both upstream zoteus and pr6012 (C2); Position One's fraction-weighted variant is deferred behind a measurement because its critic proved the naive port inverts BM25's negative-is-better scores and hands full weight to degenerate single-hit lists. idf drift from mega-documents is accepted and **disclosed** — status reports the top item's passage share and warns past 5% (graft: Position One) — not corrected: MAÎTRE D's idf epochs are rejected because FTS5's statistics are live and internal, and pinning them means reimplementing BM25 in JS (his critic's finding). Dual free win, verified today: vectors are stored L2-normalized with precomputed norms, so cosine becomes a dot product and the per-row `norm(b)` recomputation dies (graft: Five Patches' critic).

Embedder migration: vectors carry per-row embedder keys; on a model switch the queues refill while **old vectors keep serving old-model-embedded queries** — the query is dual-embedded (old + new model) during the migration window and each row is scored in its own space, then fused — closing The Ledger's availability self-contradiction exactly as its critic prescribed. Semantic search degrades gradually, never to zero, and the coverage block reports the split.

### 2.8 The contract (where the durable value lives, per C2)

Graft: MAÎTRE D's typed blocks with the prose as a rendering, corrected per his critic to **per-stage frontiers**. Every reply carries `coverage` (totals, per-stage counts, **two boundary dates** — keyword and semantic), `freshness` (both sequences, indexed vs current, `probedMsAgo`, reachability + backoff), `degradations[]` (typed `{code, effect, remedy}` — `EMBEDDER_WARMING`, `CJK_KEYWORD_DEGRADED`, `EMBEDDER_MIGRATING`…), `pending` (per-stage depths, ETA at idle priority, paused flag), `quarantined[]` (key + reason), and `scope{}` coverage inside any active filter. All counts come from **materialized counters** updated in the same transactions that change state, reconciled against a real COUNT during idle — never an O(650k) scan per reply (The Ledger's critique R2).

Verbs (graft: MAÎTRE D'): every refusal names an invocable action, because the verified corruption message tells a shell-less agent to run `rm`. `reset confirm:"DELETE_INDEX"`; `rebuild` quotes its cost and requires a count-bearing token (`confirm:"REBUILD_7541"`) so a stale confirmation can't authorize a bigger job; `prioritize{filters}` bumps frontier priority; `pause`/`resume`; `retry` for quarantines. And one consent rule (graft: Five Patches' critic C3): auto-build is default-on only for the free local embedder; API-key embedders quote the cost and require one explicit go-ahead. The machine — and the bill — belong to the user.

### 2.9 Budgets, honestly

Server steady: Node ~70MB + SQLite cache 32–64MB + query model ~120MB ≈ **~230–270MB**, target ≤300MB (the sidecar is scanned in 8MB slabs; the OS page cache, not our RSS, holds the hot copy). Worker: model ~120MB + baseline, steady ~250MB, transient peak during a monster fetch ~400–450MB, hard kill at 500MB. Disk at the design point: ~1.3GB index + ~0.7GB slabs + ~250MB sidecar ≈ **~2.3GB** — roughly 75% above upstream, the disclosed price of re-chunk-without-re-fetch and R9 streaming. Background ≤ ~1 nice'd core. Warm query: probe 0–1 request + embed 20–50ms + FTS tens of ms + scan (§3.1) + fuse ≈ **300–700ms typical**, hard budget 3s.

## 3. The seven open decisions, committed

**3.1 Semantic path at 650k vectors inside 3s.** The measured 121ms/100k is per-row-overhead-bound (~1.4GB/s effective), so the fix is **layout first, quantization second**: a contiguous derived sidecar scanned in slabs, normalized vectors so cosine is a dot, filters as a pre-scan bitmap. int8 (250MB, not Ledger Line's twice-divided 62MB) ships only through experiment **X1's gate**: recall@30 ≥ 0.98 vs exact float32 on the 0008 clustered distribution at a rerank pool ≤ 32×topK, and scan+rerank ≤ 400ms at 650k. Pass → int8 sidecar + float32 rerank from the SQLite blobs. Fail → float32 sidecar (~1GB, projected 250–500ms contiguous — still the layout win), which is also the permanent fallback. vec0 stays dead (measured non-sub-linear in k); binary stays dead (measured recall 0.26–0.86). The scan runs synchronously in the server, bounded and disclosed; a worker-thread scan is the named escape if soak shows contention. Beyond ~2M passages an ANN sidecar is an additive artifact behind the embedder key — out of scope, said out loud.

**3.2 CJK.** v1: **no keyword segmentation; the multilingual embedder is the CJK path**, and a per-query script sniff emits a typed `CJK_KEYWORD_DEGRADED` naming which list is degraded (graft: MAÎTRE D') — never silent failure, zero cost to non-CJK libraries. v2, scheduled not vague: a **trigram companion FTS table populated only for CJK-bearing passages**, back-filled from stored slabs as ordinary background work, query-routed (graft: Ledger Line). Rejected: "tokenizer swap as a key bump" (impossible — FTS5's tokenizer is table-scoped; Two-Log's critic) and Position One's bigram shadow transform (forces a whole-table contentless migration for a per-script need).

**3.3 STOPWORDS.** Deleted — but the list is the smaller half of the bug. The committed PR replaces the query tokenizer with a Unicode split (`\p{L}\p{N}+`, drop 1-char tokens, no list); FTS5 itself folds diacritics on both sides. Verified today: the current regex reduces "Việt" to `vi`,`t` and erases Cyrillic and Greek queries entirely while the index holds the text. Guard (Five Patches' critic R7): experiment X2 benches stopword-less OR-queries at 650k; if p95 keyword exceeds ~500ms, add corpus-driven pruning of query terms with document frequency above ~50% — language-neutral by construction, unlike the list it replaces. Intl.Segmenter is the deferred query-side upgrade, not a v1 dependency.

**3.4 Queue fairness vs the monster doc.** Recency-major, depth-capped two-band frontier with K=64 ≈ one median item (§2.4). Depth-major rejected on the critic's proof; strict newest-first-with-no-cap rejected on the ~19-minute monopoly arithmetic. Keyword coverage stays strictly newest-first.

**3.5 Process vs worker vs async loop per stage.** Two OS processes. Extract, chunk, embed are three ledger-paced loops inside one nice-19 worker; discovery/reconcile is an async loop in the server; query embedding is in-server at normal priority. The stage boundary is the queue, not the process; the process boundary exists exactly where nice/observe/kill needs it. Splitting embed out later is a flag, because lease-based claiming doesn't care who claims.

**3.6 Schema self-description.** The meta table is the complete derivation-key set — schema version, chunker id+geometry, embedder identity (model + revision + **E5 prefix protocol** + quantization; graft: Position One), FTS transform key, both watermarks + backend label — and the version check is **actually built** (verified missing today). Open protocol: unknown/newer schema → sideline the file (`.incompatible-<ts>`, never delete), fresh build, one notice; artifact-key mismatch → **targeted stale marks and background migration while old artifacts serve** (graft: Position One), never refusal, never silent wrongness.

**3.7 The coverage sentence.** Typed blocks (§2.8) rendered as one sentence with **per-stage boundary dates**, verbatim during a first build:

> "Searched 2,340 of 7,541 items, newest first — keyword-complete for everything added since 2019-03-14; semantic ranking covers 2,101 of those (complete since 2019-09-02; 3 oversized documents have tails still embedding). Building in background, ~40 min at idle priority. Library checked 9s ago (v412, current). 1 item quarantined: BHT7Q2 — extraction failed; retries when Zotero re-extracts it."

At steady state the sentence collapses to nothing. A single `frontierDate` is rejected as false by construction (MAÎTRE D's critic): keyword and vector frontiers provably diverge, so both dates are stated.

**Rejected outright, for the record:** queues as pure views (Ledger; its critic's arithmetic); pipeline co-resident with the server and DB-row scheduling signals (Ledger; verified synchronous driver + priority inversion); four processes and self-SIGSTOP (Ledger Line; isolation tax, Unix-only complexity); storing no extracted text (Two-Log; re-chunk = re-download); bge-m3/1024-dim (dimension trap); idf epochs (BM25 reimplementation); fraction-weighted RRF as specified (sign trap, degenerate fractions — deferred behind measurement); inline delta application on the query path (8s inside 3s); uncapped auto-build on paid embedders (consent); vec0, binary quantization, and the JSON backend at the design point (all measured dead by the sheet).

## 5. The three biggest remaining risks, and the cheapest falsifiers

**Risk 1 — the vector numbers are still projections.** int8 recall at small pools was never measured (binary needed 8–16k-candidate pools to recover recall); the contiguous-layout speedup in Node against this exact data is inferred, not shown. *Cheapest falsifier:* X1 — one afternoon on the existing 0008 harness with 100k real vectors. If int8 misses the gate and float32 slabs still exceed ~800ms at 650k, the R8 story degrades to "linear, disclosed, ~1s" — still inside 3s, but the headroom claim dies and the doc gets rewritten before any code does.

**Risk 2 — the maintainer reimplements the core.** PR9–11 are precisely issue-#10-shaped, and pr6012 is moving upstream underneath us; a fork that diverges on machinery while Zotero ships native embeddings could be stranded twice. *Cheapest falsifier:* the RFC issue itself, opened after PR1–8 establish credibility — his response in one issue thread settles fork-vs-upstream for the cost of writing it. The hedge is already structural: every stage sits behind a key, so his machinery under our contract is an acceptable ending.

**Risk 3 — the synchronous driver under real concurrency.** Two writers (tick + worker) and a reading query path on `DatabaseSync` is designed-safe (short transactions, write-free queries, WAL) but unproven; WAL growth under a long drain and the tail of `busy_timeout` are exactly where designs like this die quietly. *Cheapest falsifier:* a scripted soak — full pipeline drain on the 10k-item corpus plus one query per second for 30 minutes; assert reply p95 ≤ 1.5s, no busy-wait over 100ms, WAL ≤ 256MB with checkpoints landing. If it fails, the named fallback (single dedicated writer connection in the worker, smaller commit batches) is a contained change, not a redesign.

The bet, in one sentence: the ledger makes every failure boring, the contract makes every answer honest, the frontier makes partial useful from minute one — and the only claims left unproven are the three above, each falsifiable for less than a day's work before the expensive code exists.
